Research

My research lies at the intersection of geometric group theory and probability. I study how the geometry of a group helps describe the asymptotic behavior of random walks, with a particular focus on groups exhibiting hyperbolic features.

Martin boundary and geometric boundaries

The Martin boundary connects random walks with positive harmonic functions. My work investigates how it can be understood using geometric boundaries and geodesic rays.

In joint work with Wenyuan Yang, we construct an embedding of a subset of full Patterson–Sullivan measure of the Roller boundary into the minimal Martin boundary for a class of right-angled Coxeter groups satisfying specific hypotheses. For geometric actions with contracting elements, we obtain a related boundary map from a full-measure subset of the horofunction boundary, with injectivity up to finite-difference equivalence and continuity in a direct limit topology.

Ancona inequalities and Morse geometry

Ancona inequalities compare Green functions along geodesics, providing a key tool for relating probabilistic and geometric boundaries. Our work establishes such inequalities along Morse geodesics and studies extensions involving Morse subsets with narrow points.

For irreducible, finitely supported random walks on non-amenable groups, these results yield an injective map from the Morse boundary into the Martin boundary, continuous with respect to the direct limit topology on the Morse boundary.

Geometric settings and methods

The geometry of CAT(0) cube complexes, Morse hyperplanes, and contracting elements plays a central role in this work. In suitable settings, we construct families of geodesic rays converging to Martin boundary points and study the resulting maps between boundaries.

The precise assumptions and statements are given in our preprint, Ancona inequalities along generic geodesic rays.
